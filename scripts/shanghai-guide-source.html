Guide to Obtaining a Certificate of No Criminal Record in Shanghai, China

Learn how foreigners can obtain a Shanghai police clearance certificate, including eligibility, required documents, application procedures, notarization and apostille.

By: China Attest

Last Updated: 26 November 2026

1. Overview

Foreign nationals who have lived in Shanghai may need to obtain a Certificate of No Criminal Record for various purposes, including immigration, employment, and other overseas applications.

The certificate is also commonly referred to as a police certificate, police check, or police clearance certificate in other countries.

The issuance of Certificates of No Criminal Record in Shanghai is governed by the Provisions on the Handling of Criminal Record Check by Public Security Organs, which came into effect on December 31, 2021.

For overseas use, the certificate generally needs to be notarized by a local Chinese notary office. Depending on the destination country, the notarized document may also require an apostille issued by China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs or consular legalization by the relevant embassy.

This guide explains the eligibility requirements, required documents, application procedures, and notarization process for foreign nationals seeking a Shanghai Certificate of No Criminal Record.

2. Eligibility

Foreign nationals applying for a Certificate of No Criminal Record in Shanghai must meet the following requirements:

  • Must have held residence permits issued in Shanghai.
  • Must have lived in Shanghai for 180 days or longer.

Applicants should ensure that their residence history and supporting documents can demonstrate their eligibility.

3. Required Documents

Step 1: Application for a Certificate of No Criminal Record

The following documents are required:

  • Passport(s): Relevant passport documents covering the applicant's residence history in Shanghai.
  • Power of Attorney: Required if a third party is appointed to handle the application on the applicant's behalf.

If the applicant is living outside China, the Power of Attorney must be either:

  • Apostilled by the competent foreign affairs authority in the country where the applicant resides; or
  • Authenticated by a Chinese embassy or consulate in that country.

Step 2: Notarization of the Certificate of No Criminal Record

The following documents are required:

  • Passport(s).
  • Certificate of No Criminal Record: The original certificate issued by the Shanghai Public Security Bureau or its relevant branch.
  • Power of Attorney: Required if a third party is appointed to handle the notarization application.

4. Application Process

Obtaining a Shanghai Certificate of No Criminal Record for overseas use generally involves two main steps.

Step 1: Apply for a Certificate of No Criminal Record

Eligible foreign nationals can submit their applications to the Division of Exit and Entry Administration of the Shanghai Public Security Bureau or the exit and entry administration branch in the district where they reside.

Applicants should prepare their passports and, where applicable, a Power of Attorney.

Processing Time: Approximately 2 weeks.

Step 2: Obtain Notarization of the Certificate

After receiving the Certificate of No Criminal Record, applicants can submit it to a local notary office for the issuance of a Notarial Certificate of No Criminal Record.

The notarized certificate is generally the document required for overseas purposes such as immigration, employment, and school attendance.

Shanghai has more than 20 notary offices licensed to handle foreign-related notarization.

A complete list of their contact information is available through the Shanghai 12348 Public Legal Service Platform.

Processing Time: Approximately 1–2 weeks.

Additional Authentication for Overseas Use

Depending on the destination country, the notarized document may also need:

  • Apostille: Issued by China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs or an authorized office.
  • Consular Legalization: Authentication by the relevant embassy when applicable.

The required authentication procedure depends on the destination country's document requirements.

5. Application Authority

Certificate of No Criminal Record

Applications can be submitted to:

Division of Exit and Entry Administration, Shanghai Public Security Bureau.

Alternatively, applicants may contact the exit and entry administration branch in the district of their residence.

Notarization

Notarization can be handled by a local notary office in Shanghai that is licensed to provide foreign-related notarization services.

The Shanghai 12348 Public Legal Service Platform provides a complete list of relevant notary offices and their contact information.

6. Special Requirements

Power of Attorney for Applicants Outside China

If an applicant is living outside China and appoints a third party to handle the application, the Power of Attorney must be properly authenticated.

It must either be apostilled by the competent foreign affairs authority in the applicant's country or authenticated by a Chinese embassy or consulate in that country, as applicable.

Sample Notarial Certificate of No Criminal Record

According to the original information provided for this guide, notary offices across China issue Notarial Certificates of No Criminal Record in a consistent format.

For more information, please refer to the related article:

Notarization for Overseas Use

A Certificate of No Criminal Record is generally not sufficient for overseas immigration, employment, or educational applications without notarization.

Applicants should check the document requirements of their destination country to determine whether an apostille or consular legalization is also necessary.

7. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Where can foreigners apply for a Shanghai Certificate of No Criminal Record?

Applications can be submitted to the Division of Exit and Entry Administration of the Shanghai Public Security Bureau or the relevant district exit and entry administration branch where the applicant resides.

What are the eligibility requirements?

Foreign nationals must have held residence permits issued in Shanghai and must have lived in Shanghai for at least 180 days.

How long does the application take?

The Certificate of No Criminal Record application generally takes approximately 2 weeks.

Notarization generally takes an additional 1–2 weeks.

Is notarization required for overseas use?

Generally, yes. The certificate usually needs to be notarized by a local Chinese notary office before it can be used for overseas immigration, employment, or educational purposes.

Does the notarized certificate also need an apostille?

This depends on the destination country. Some countries require an apostille, while others may require consular legalization.

Can someone apply on my behalf?

A third party may be appointed to handle the application using a Power of Attorney. If the applicant is living outside China, the Power of Attorney must be apostilled or authenticated by a Chinese embassy or consulate, as applicable.
